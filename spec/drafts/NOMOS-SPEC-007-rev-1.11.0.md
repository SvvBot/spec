# NOMOS-SPEC-007 Revision 1.11.0: Statement Authority Comes From the Root

**Status:** Draft (proposed revision)

**Proposed:** 2026-11-12

## Abstract

Mechanizing chain resolution in Lean ([`papers/carried-authority/lean/Chain.lean`](../../papers/carried-authority/lean/Chain.lean))
proved no amplification (Part II, Proposition 2) and complete revocation (Proposition 3), and
found two gaps. Both were reproduced against the reference resolvers before this revision.

1. **A sealing key could certify witnesses and approvers.** Rev. 1.8 §3.5 separates purposes per
   key: a sealing key cannot testify or consent. But a key with no `claim` or `consent` term
   could certify one that has them, because a dimension absent from the parent constrains
   nothing when checking a child's scope. So one stolen sealing key could supply the rules, the
   facts those rules read, and approvers for as many "people" as an escalation required. Part II
   Theorem 9 (items 3, 4 and 6) and Part IV's "one stolen key is one vote" assume this cannot
   happen.
2. **Presentation order could decide the reported failure.** Candidates are ordered by a
   fingerprint that excludes the signature, so two certificates differing only in signature tie,
   and the order they were presented in decided which failure was reported. Part II Proposition
   4 says the verdict depends on the set, never on its order.

The rest of rev. 1.10 is unchanged.

## §3.5 Key purpose (amended, normative)

Added to rev. 1.8 §3.5:

> A `claim` or `consent` term MAY appear on a certificate whose parent is the pinned root. On any
> other certificate it MAY appear only if the parent's effective scope already contains a term of
> the same dimension (which the certificate then narrows, per §3.4). Otherwise the candidate fails
> with `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, the dimension, and `reason_code: "statement_below_root"`.

Statement authority is therefore granted by the root and only narrowed below it. A key that may
seal, having no statement term, can never be an ancestor of a witness or an approver; an
institution delegates statement authority through a root-certified statement key (an enroller
carrying `consent:<roles>`, a witness authority carrying `claim:<facts>`). Proved in
`Chain.lean` (`statement_authority_from_root`).

## §4.2a step 2 (amended, normative)

> Certificates with the same fingerprint are ordered by their `signature` string, ascending.

## Vectors

[`chain-of-trust-vectors/vectors-1.11.json`](../../chain-of-trust-vectors/vectors-1.11.json), 41 cases,
generated by `generate_111.py`: rev. 1.9's cases with witness and approver keys certified the
rev. 1.11 way, plus `sealing_key_cannot_certify_a_witness`, `sealing_key_cannot_certify_an_approver`,
`unscoped_intermediate_cannot_certify_an_approver` and `same_payload_certificates_in_every_order`.
Run against a rev. 1.10 resolver, exactly those four fail. `vectors-1.9.json` is kept as the record
of rev. 1.9; five of its cases certify statement keys under an unscoped intermediate and are
refused under this revision.

## Compatibility

A deployment that certifies every witness and approver key directly under its root, or through a
root-certified key carrying the same statement dimension, is unaffected. The NOMOS platform
issues keys this way. A deployment that certifies statement keys under a sealing key or an
unscoped intermediate must reissue those certificates.
